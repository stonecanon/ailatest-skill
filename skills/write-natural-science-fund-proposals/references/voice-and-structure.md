# Voice And Structure

## Voice

Match the user's national-fund writing style without mentioning the reference document in the proposal:

- Formal, compact, mechanism-oriented, and evidence-led.
- Dense enough for a grant, but organized by explicit causal and logical relations.
- Specific about research objects, observations, variables, uncertainty sources, propagation paths, and validation.
- Restrained about claims; avoid slogans and promotional language.
- Consistent in terminology across title, questions, content, methods, figures, and outcomes.

Use paragraph transitions that make the scientific argument visible:

1. Identify the limitation, contradiction, or unresolved mechanism.
2. Define the research object and boundary.
3. State the proposed representation, model, or experiment.
4. Explain how the mechanism will be identified or tested.
5. State the scientific output or boundary that will result.

## Preferred Language

Prefer concrete verbs:

- `构建` a data base, sample system, graph, model, or framework.
- `表征` uncertainty, state, prior, error, or multimodal observation.
- `解析` propagation relations, action paths, and causal chains.
- `识别` dominant links, prior boundaries, risk areas, or state changes.
- `量化` uncertainty, effect size, sensitivity, confidence, and error parameters.
- `揭示` propagation mechanisms and scientific laws.
- `验证` mechanisms, models, and scenario applicability.
- `界定` valid boundaries and applicability conditions.

Avoid vague verbs such as `赋能`, `打造`, `全面提升`, or `形成闭环` unless the exact mechanism is stated.

## Three Research Contents

Keep these established content roles unless the user changes the project:

1. `图模约束数据基座与统一样本构建`
2. `不确定性分层表征与传播机制建模`
3. `主动观测优化与机制验证`

For each content block, write one coherent expanded paragraph or subsection:

- Start with the scientific gap addressed by the block.
- Define inputs, objects, or state variables.
- Explain the mechanism or relation being studied.
- End with the output that supports the next block.

Do not turn research-content paragraphs into lists of software, sensors, or algorithms. Move those details to the research scheme.

## Two Scientific Questions

Keep the established roles:

1. Unified representation and propagation of embodied-perception uncertainty under drawing/model constraints.
2. Active reduction of uncertainty for trustworthy state discrimination.

Use the same core wording everywhere. Do not create additional quasi-questions in objectives, innovations, or figures.

## Research Scheme

Expand the same three blocks with methods:

- Block one: drawing/model standardization, constraint graph, multimodal synchronous sensing, coordinate/time calibration, sample annotation, confidence calibration.
- Block two: layered uncertainty representation, propagation chain, Bayesian or probabilistic modeling, parameter identification, sensitivity analysis, prior-validity boundary.
- Block three: risk recognition, candidate path/view selection, information-gain re-observation, posterior update, comparison experiments, scenario validation.

Explain why each method answers its corresponding scientific question. Do not merely name algorithms.

## Limitations And Innovation

Write three limitations that align one-to-one with the three contents:

1. Fragmented drawing/model and observation data; lack of unified samples.
2. Unclear layered uncertainty propagation and prior-validity boundary.
3. Passive observation cannot actively reduce uncertainty for trustworthy state decisions.

Write innovations as scientific advances over these limitations, not as a feature list.

## Line And Paragraph Quality

- Avoid a final visual line with one Chinese character.
- Avoid headings followed by text on the same line when the heading is meant to stand alone.
- Keep terms such as `图模约束`, `具身感知`, `不确定性传播`, `主动观测`, and `可信状态判别` stable.
- Remove duplicate summary sentences across research content, objectives, and innovations.
