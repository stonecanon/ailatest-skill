# Figure Patterns

## Figure 1: Research Background And Object Boundary

Compose four linked zones:

1. Urban or engineering demand and scene pain points.
2. Drawing/model prior plus embodied sensing platform.
3. Trustworthy spatial-state outputs.
4. Scenario coverage boundary.

Use a real city/construction image and a real sensing-platform image when available. Express pain points as three concise items. Show outputs such as risk area, confidence, and update recommendation. Keep scenario cards compact.

## Figure 2: Scientific Questions And Research-Content Relationship

Use a composition that differs from the full technical route:

- Put three research contents in a vertical progression.
- Place scientific question one beside contents one/two.
- Place scientific question two beside contents two/three.
- Use symmetrical branch connectors.
- Close with one research-organization statement.

Do not repeat method panels or annual-plan material here. Keep content detail concise and remove tool lists.

## Figure 3: Data Flow And Unified Sample Construction

Use five stages:

1. Multi-source input: AutoCAD drawings, BIM/IFC, completion records, point clouds.
2. Drawing/model parsing: planar boundary, spatial topology, component semantics.
3. Constraint graph: topology graph, semantic graph, tolerance graph.
4. Embodied-observation synchronization: unified time-space reference, geometry, people, environment, pose/time.
5. Unified sample: model prior, pose state, multimodal observation, spatial state, confidence.

Add a compact quality-control band for coordinate unification, time synchronization, error calibration, state annotation, and confidence calibration. Keep icons visually distinct and all panels on one consistent blue-white hierarchy.

## Figure 4: Active Observation Loop And Evaluation

Use a mathematically exact circular loop for:

1. Prior risk.
2. Candidate view/path.
3. Multimodal observation.
4. State update.
5. Re-observation ranking.

Pair the loop with a validation panel containing a compact evaluation matrix and charts. Label x and y axes. Use blue, green, and orange/red series consistently. Keep feedback and parameter-calibration links centered and symmetric.

## Figure 5: Technical Route

Use this hierarchy:

1. Project title.
2. Three research-content columns.
3. Three corresponding method panels.
4. Two scientific questions.
5. Three target outcomes.
6. One final application/service sentence.

Do not include the annual plan. Use real evidence images in the method layer when useful. Keep research-content boxes free of method/tool lists. Make the final application sentence large and visually full, but not a second title.

## Cross-Figure Consistency

- Keep the same three content names across all figures and正文.
- Keep the same two scientific-question formulations across all figures and正文.
- Keep outcome wording consistent with the expected-results section.
- Ensure every figure reference and caption matches the actual figure.
- Rebuild all affected figures when content wording changes; do not update only one diagram.
